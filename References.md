## Primary philosophical texts

1. **Penrose, Roger. *The Road to Reality: A Complete Guide to the Laws of the Universe*. Jonathan Cape, 2004.** Chapter 1, especially §1.4, “Three worlds and three deep mysteries,” pp. 17–21 and figure 1.3. [Chapter PDF hosted by the University of São Paulo](https://astroweb.iag.usp.br/~amancio/mpa5003_notas/road-to-reality-roger-penrose.pdf). The relevant section was inspected. Mathematical formulations are included in the working category of Descriptions; the status of mathematical objects and structures remains open. The category is not identified with Penrose’s Platonic mathematical world.

2. **Popper, Karl R. “Three Worlds.” The Tanner Lecture on Human Values, delivered at the University of Michigan, 7 April 1978.** [Lecture text from the Tanner Lectures](https://tannerlectures.org/documents/three-worlds/). The text was inspected, especially sections I, X, XIV, and XVIII. Relevant to the distinction between subjective thinking and objective thought contents, and to the proposed relationships among the realms. Babak has adopted “Descriptions” as a working name for objective thought contents with Popper’s broader cultural scope. This does not endorse Popper’s realism or settle every boundary case. See the [dialogue](Dialogs/2026-11-27-frameworks-and-descriptions.md). Historical priority remains open.

## Greek precedents for Descriptions

These sources support the [exploratory memo](Memos/2026-11-27-greek-precedents-for-descriptions.md). They establish relevant comparisons, not the exact three-domain division or a demonstrated line of influence.

- **Plato. *Republic* X, 596a–598b.** [Benjamin Jowett's English translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Republic_of_Plato/Book_10). Primary text inspected: Form, manufactured bed, and painted image. Relevant to intelligible standards and representations; not an ancient version of the Physical World / Human Experience / Descriptions division.
- **Aristotle. *On Interpretation* 1, 16a3–8.** [E. M. Edghill's English translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/interpretation.1.1.html). Primary text inspected: things, affections of the soul, spoken sounds, and written marks. The translation's “mental experience” must not be assumed to coincide with Malakut's entire experiential domain.
- **Sextus Empiricus. *Against the Mathematicians* VIII.11–12.** Ancient testimony to Stoic doctrine, inspected as an English excerpt reproduced in [University of Vermont course notes](https://www.uvm.edu/~jbailly/courses/196Stoicism/notes/StoicLogic.html), under “Philosophy of Language.” Distinguishes utterance, signified sayable, and external object. The excerpt's translator is not identified on that page; a critical edition and fully attributed translation remain to be checked before direct quotation in formal writing. The surrounding course notes are secondary commentary, not Sextus's text.
- **“Stoicism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, §3.2, “Philosophy of Language and Lekta.”** [Scholarly overview](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/). Inspected to clarify the scope of sayables and their distinction from physical linguistic expressions. Used as commentary, not as an ancient primary text.
