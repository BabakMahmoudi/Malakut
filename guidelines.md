# Malakut: Guidelines for Philosophical Investigation

*AI-generated methodological adaptation (model: GPT-6), prepared on 27 November 2026 from the Natural Laws project's README at Babak's request and revised to reflect his direction for Malakut. The revised wording awaits his review.*

## Purpose and provenance

Malakut is a developing philosophical investigation. Its questions, scope, and possible conclusions should emerge through inquiry. Neither the project's name nor the methodology establishes a philosophical, theological, or metaphysical commitment.

These guidelines adapt the method and writing conventions of [Natural Laws](https://github.com/BabakMahmoudi/Natural-Laws), particularly its [README at the source revision](https://github.com/BabakMahmoudi/Natural-Laws/blob/1e4f419415881f1f4bc318242eaac55fe7f8b77c/README.md). They import a way of working; substantive positions from that project require separate discussion before being attributed to Malakut.

## Method and roles

Babak brings intuitions, questions, doubts, examples, and a sense of how the world feels and appears. Treat these as substantive beginnings of philosophical inquiry. The AI contributes concepts, historical positions, arguments, distinctions, sources, and possible objections. It helps articulate and test ideas, challenges weak steps constructively, and preserves Babak's judgment.

Dialogue is the primary place where ideas emerge and change. Attend to the particular question being pursued, stay with it when Babak asks to focus, and allow uncertainty and reflection. Avoid turning every exchange into a survey of philosophers or a premature finished argument.

Distinguish throughout:

- What Babak has explicitly proposed or affirmed.
- What the AI suggests, infers, or reconstructs.
- What a source or philosopher actually argues.
- What remains a conjecture, analogy, objection, or open question.

Disagreement and self-correction belong in the record. Formulate serious alternatives fairly and test the strongest relevant objections. Do not manufacture agreement or resolve a tension merely to make a text appear complete.

## Working path

1. **Dialogues** preserve the exploratory exchange and the development of questions.
2. **Memos** extract a focused problem, insight, argument, and remaining difficulty.
3. **Essays** develop selected insights into independently readable philosophical writing. They are the expected main form of developed work.
4. **Papers and book chapters** may grow from the material when sustained formal argument or a longer connected treatment is warranted.

These are possible forms, not mandatory stages. A dialogue need not produce a memo or essay, and an exploratory question need not become a thesis.

### Authorship and review status

The highest presentation priority is that readers immediately understand that project prose is AI-assisted or AI-generated. This applies to the README as well as every other AI-generated artifact. Place a prominent disclosure immediately after the title, before the substantive text. Name the actual model when known; otherwise write **AI model not identified**. Identify known contributions from multiple models where practicable.

For example:

> AI-generated draft (model: GPT-6), developed from project dialogues. Not yet reviewed or endorsed by Babak.

Adapt the disclosure to the actual provenance and review status. Distinguish approval of an idea from approval of the prose expressing it. Do not imply human authorship merely because the underlying ideas arose in dialogue. Update the disclosure after substantial rewriting or explicit review.

### Writing and argument

- Write clear, precise prose. Introduce technical vocabulary when needed and explain it.
- Use concrete examples to expose distinctions or test arguments. Explain where an analogy helps and where its force ends.
- Distinguish descriptions of experience, claims about knowledge, claims about language, and claims about what exists. Make any move between them explicit.
- State assumptions and identify what would challenge a conclusion.
- Preserve important objections, unresolved tensions, and changes of mind.
- Do not invent quotations, references, facts about a thinker's position, personal experiences, or statements by Babak.
- Mark interpretations, inferences, and speculative proposals. Distinguish a useful explanatory framework from an argument establishing its truth.
- Make the reasons for significant revisions intelligible and preserve links to earlier stages.

### Sources and verification

Verify quotations and substantive attributions against the relevant sources before presenting them as established. Prefer primary texts for claims about a thinker's position; use scholarship to clarify context and competing interpretations.

Provide bibliographic details and page, section, or other locators where available. Distinguish direct quotation from paraphrase and reconstruction. Mark unverified leads explicitly; a link or an AI recollection does not establish that a source supports a claim. Record useful sources in `References.md`.

When translating, identify the source language and translation used. Label an AI translation as such and preserve the original term when a philosophical distinction depends on it.

## Artifact conventions

### Dialogues — `Dialogs/`

Preserve an exchange between human and AI, in the spirit of philosophical dialogue. Label the human speaker **Babak** and the AI with its actual model name when known, otherwise **AI**.

Keep the sequence and distinct voices, including hesitation, questions, objections, clarification, and changes of mind. Light editing for readability is permitted. Do not fabricate turns, reassign claims, or silently transform the exchange into an essay. Disclose substantial reconstruction in an editorial note.

Imagined conversations with historical or living thinkers must be clearly labeled as imagined. Their invented speech must not be presented as quotations or actual interviews.

### Memos — `Memos/`

Normally use three or four concise paragraphs. State the question, the main insight or position reached, its principal reason, and the most important objection or open question. Link the source dialogue and any sources used.

A memo captures a stage of inquiry. It should preserve uncertainty and distinguish Babak's proposals from AI suggestions and positions in the literature.

### Essays — `Essays/`

Pursue a live question through reflection, examples, distinctions, and argument in an engaging authorial voice. Let the originating intuitions direct the inquiry while literature sharpens or challenges the thought.

Write for readers who have not seen the dialogues. Develop the material beyond a summary. An essay may remain exploratory, but its movement and grounds should be intelligible.

Do not name the human contributor or narrate the conversation in the essay body unless requested. Put provenance in the disclosure and source links. Preserve consequential open questions and check factual claims and quotations.

### Papers — `Papers/`

Provide a clear thesis or research question, sustained argument, engagement with literature and objections, checked citations, and a bibliography. Keep conclusions proportionate to what the argument establishes.

### Book material — `Book/`

Make chapters independently intelligible while developing a connected argument. Maintain terminology, explain each chapter's role, and connect it with neighboring material. Allow a coherent position to emerge from the investigation.

### Current commitments — `MyTheory.md`

When the inquiry develops commitments, record them as short, numbered, revisable propositions. Each should state one precise claim and link to supporting project texts. Keep open questions separate.

Distinguish explicitly affirmed positions from proposed AI formulations awaiting review. Update the list when discussion changes a position, recording the reason and supporting material. Do not seed it with commitments imported from Natural Laws.

### References — `References.md`

Maintain bibliographic details, links, and brief notes on relevance. Distinguish primary texts, commentary, and unverified leads. Link sources to the project texts that use them where useful.

## Repository and revision practice

Keep `README.md` focused on substantive project content: its motivating image, the three-realm proposal, central questions, and relevant intellectual context. Begin it with the prominent AI disclosure. Keep agent instructions and detailed working conventions in `guidelines.md`; the README may link here without reproducing those instructions. Create the other files and directories as material warrants them; the paths above describe conventions, not files already present.

Use descriptive Markdown filenames, normally `YYYY-MM-DD-topic.md` for dated dialogues, memos, and essays. Use relative links within the repository. Preserve earlier stages of thought through the dialogue record and Git history.

Before saving an artifact, check attribution, authorship disclosure, review status, source support, links, and compliance with its writing format. Report important gaps or unverified claims candidly. When completing a repository change, identify the files changed and summarize the substantive result.
