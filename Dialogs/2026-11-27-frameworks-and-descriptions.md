# Three Domains of Discourse and the Meaning of Descriptions

**AI-assisted dialogue between Babak Mahmoudi and GPT-6, edited and abridged by GPT-6 on 27 November 2026. Babak affirmed the working distinctions recorded here; this edited presentation awaits his review.**

*Editorial note: This record follows the actual exchange in sequence. Babak's turns are lightly edited for spelling and readability. The AI replies are substantially abridged and edited, rather than verbatim transcripts. Progress messages and tool activity are omitted. No invented turns have been added.*

## Existence claims and frameworks

**Babak:** Claims of existence need a framework for existence claims. Hmm. I mean, first we should clarify what I mean by existence. I prefer the Carnapian approach. The three realms claimed are a convenience in language for addressing them. What are other ways of tackling existence claims?

**GPT-6:** I take you to mean that the three realms are a convenience in language: a way to distinguish and discuss physical reality, human experience, and descriptions. Calling them realms need not already commit you to three independently existing worlds.

We should distinguish the claim that an intelligible existence claim needs concepts and rules from the claim that those concepts and rules determine whether something exists. The first does not establish the second.

Carnap distinguishes existence questions within a linguistic framework from the practical question of adopting it. Internal questions can be answered through evidence or logic; their answers are not simply arbitrary conventions.[1]

Other approaches include metaphysical realism; Quine's examination of theoretical commitments; phenomenological inquiry into how things appear; ontological pluralism; fictionalism; and inquiry into dependence and fundamentality. Quine's criterion identifies what a theory says exists, rather than proving that those entities exist.[2] For Malakut, we might provisionally introduce three domains of discourse while leaving their ontological interpretation open.

## A distinction for inquiry

**Babak:** Yes, I agree. I am not in a position to claim metaphysical entities. I am also not a fan of Quine's commitment, for reasons I stated elsewhere. Dependence and fundamentality are also part of the project's inquiry. I think that, for now, the triple division is only a claim of a convenient discourse. Later we will come back to deeper ontology.

**GPT-6:** Then our starting point is a threefold distinction for organizing discourse, with its ontological interpretation left open:

> We distinguish the physical world, human experience, and descriptions as three useful domains of discourse. Introducing this distinction does not itself assert three separate metaphysical entities or independently existing realms. Their relationships—including dependence, emergence, and fundamentality—are subjects of the investigation. Deeper ontological commitments may arise later and will require separate arguments.

The division can be revised if it proves unhelpful. “Three realms of existence” is stronger than the present commitment warrants. “Three domains of discourse” expresses it more accurately.

## Penrose and Popper

**Babak:** Okay, now let us focus on descriptions. For the other two—the physical and experience—it seems that we, Popper, and Penrose stand almost at the same point. But for the third, Penrose says mathematics and Popper says objective thoughts. These seem different.

**GPT-6:** Their third worlds differ in scope and in what makes their contents objective. The physical and mental categories broadly correspond, although this does not establish agreement about their relationships.

Penrose treats mathematical reality as exceeding what any individual, or all mathematicians together, happens to think. It is more than mathematical notation or our formulations.[3]

Popper's World 3 includes mathematical constructions, scientific theories, problems, stories, languages, music, and other cultural products. Objective thought content is distinguished from the particular experience of thinking it. A theory can have implications its author never considered. “Objective” does not mean “true”: an erroneous theory can still have content available for criticism.[4]

Consider a printed theorem, your experience of understanding it, its formulation, and the mathematical relationship it concerns. Penrose's distinctive commitment concerns mathematical reality beyond our formulations. Popper emphasizes intellectual contents and their logical relationships within a broader account of human cultural products.

Our word “descriptions” needs clarification: in ordinary usage it is narrower than Popper's World 3, since a symphony or a command need not describe anything. Whether mathematical structures are descriptions, or something mathematical descriptions describe, is a further question.

## Descriptions as objective thought contents

**Babak:** Yes, I think I should take Popper's boat. I think I am aligned with his broader scope. For now, let's assume “description” is my name for Popper's objective thought content.

**GPT-6:** Then let us use “Descriptions” as your working name for Popper's objective thought contents, with the broader scope you prefer.

The term includes theories, mathematical formulations, stories, and other articulated intellectual or cultural contents. It distinguishes the content we can share, interpret, and criticize from a particular person's experience of thinking it and from its physical expression.

> **Descriptions:** shareable thought contents, understood broadly in the spirit of Popper's World 3. The term includes more than descriptions in the ordinary sense and does not imply that its contents are true.

Your adoption concerns the scope of the category. Whether these contents exist independently, how they depend on experience and physical embodiment, and whether Popper's realism is justified remain questions for Malakut.
